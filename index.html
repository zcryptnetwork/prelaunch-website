01 — 06 Why this is different

01

Cash is
a file.

A payment is a small text file. Send it on WhatsApp, email, AirDrop, or on paper. The person you give it to drops it into their wallet and claims it. No address. No account. They don't need to own any coins to start.

zcrypt-bearer.txt
zcrypt-bearer/1
coin zcb1q8f3l…m7d2kx0v
gas  zcb1zr9ap…4hqn6te8

Until it is claimed, any copy of the file is still worth the money. The first claim wins. Send it like cash, then delete your copy.

02

Nobody sees
who paid whom.

On most blockchains every payment is public forever. A ZCrypt payment isn't, and there is no public mode you could forget to switch off. All coins, of every kind, sit in one crowd, and a payment cannot be picked out of it.

A payment never shows the sender, the recipient, the amount, or the coin. Staking, trade escrows, and the Bitcoin bridge are public by design.

03

Hide in the whole crowd,
not a small one.

Other privacy coins hide you among a handful of decoys, and over time patterns leak through. On ZCrypt every payment hides among every coin that has ever existed. The crowd only gets bigger, and it never thins out.

Even the number of coins you spent is hidden.

04

That file can hold
your own coin, too.

Pick a name, a precision, and a supply, and you have a currency. From then on it is sent, traded, and handed over as a file exactly like ZCRYPT, with exactly the same privacy, and nobody has to list it anywhere. On the chain, a payment in your coin and a payment in ZCRYPT look identical.

05

Trade without
an exchange.

No company has to list your coin, and no company can freeze it or pull the plug. Two people agree on a trade, each locks what they're offering, and both sides settle together or not at all. ZCRYPT for any coin on the chain. Bitcoin, too.

The escrow for a trade is public: it names that trade's amount and coin so both sides can see what they lock. Everything before and after it is a private payment.

06

Don't trust us.
Check it.

Private money needs public rules. Every payment carries proof that it passed seven simple checks, and you can watch those checks on any transaction in the explorer. The rule that nobody can create money out of thin air has been checked line by line by a proof assistant, not just promised.

  1. Amounts are real
  2. One coin type per payment
  3. Nothing created, nothing lost
  4. Spent coins are spent
  5. New coins match
  6. Every coin came from the pool
  7. Done

Open any transaction in the explorer and watch the checks run.

How these were measured: medians on one Apple M3 Max laptop (14 cores, 36 GB), release build, the shipped parameter set of 112 FRI queries, over 12 proving runs and 40 verification runs per configuration. One proof takes 107 ms to make and 10 ms to check; a payment is two proofs. Each proof is about 1 MB and a full payment is about 4 MB on chain, which is why bandwidth, not proving, sets the network's limit. Details and the throughput caveats are in the paper, section "Measured Performance".

Built for the quantum era

The coins and the proofs rest on hash and lattice cryptography, the post-quantum standards being adopted now, so a payment recorded today is not waiting to be opened later. Two parts stay classical and sit outside the pool: block signing and the Bitcoin bridge. Neither can forge a coin.

Show your accountant. Only your accountant.

You can hand someone a view-only key that reveals what came into one of your addresses, and nothing more. They can't spend. They can't see what you did next. Move to a new address and the key sees nothing further.

Honest about the edges

Staking, trade escrows, and the Bitcoin bridge are public by design. The proofs cover the money rules, not every line of the system, and there has been no outside audit yet. Everything that is and isn't private, and is and isn't proven, is written down in plain language.

Download Walk me through it

Checking the latest release.

    1. 1
      Install it. Mac: open the disk image. Windows: run the setup. Linux: use the AppImage or the deb.
    2. 2
      Connect to the network. Settings → Consensus Node → https://rpc.zcrypt.network.
    3. 3
      Tor is on by default. If you don't run Tor, switch it off in Settings for your first session.
    4. 4
      Get paid with nothing in your wallet. Someone sends you coins or a bearer file, you hit Refresh, and it's yours. Each payment you send costs 0.01 ZCRYPT.

    Mac builds are signed and notarized by Apple. The Windows installer is not yet signed, so SmartScreen may warn you. Want to build it yourself? The developer docs show how.

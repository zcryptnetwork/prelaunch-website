Post-quantum

Post-quantum from the first block.

ZCrypt payments use STARK proofs, hash-based ownership, ML-KEM encryption and ML-DSA signatures. There is no elliptic curve anywhere in a payment, so there is nothing in one for a quantum computer to break.

The stack

Four post-quantum parts. No elliptic curves.

STARKProofsEvery payment is proven with a hash-based zero-knowledge proof. No trusted setup, no pairing, no curve.
Poseidon2Ownership and coinsYou own a coin by knowing a hash preimage. Coins, spends and the tree are hashes. A payment carries no signature.
ML-KEM-768EncryptionPayment details are encrypted with the NIST post-quantum standard, FIPS 203.
ML-DSA-65SignaturesCoin issuers sign with the NIST post-quantum standard, FIPS 204.

Bitcoin, Ethereum, Zcash and Monero rest on elliptic curves. A large quantum computer breaks elliptic curves. ZCrypt payments do not use them.

01

What a quantum computer breaks.

Two quantum algorithms matter for money.

  • Shor's algorithm solves the mathematics behind elliptic-curve and RSA cryptography outright. With a large enough machine, a public key gives up its private key. Bitcoin, Ethereum, Zcash and Monero all rest on elliptic curves: for signatures, and in the private ones for hiding amounts and building proofs.
  • Grover's algorithm speeds up brute-force search. It does not break a hash function; it halves its strength in bits. A hash that is wide enough stays safe.

So the rule is simple: use hashes that are wide enough, and nothing built on elliptic curves.

02

What ZCrypt uses instead.

WhatHow ZCrypt does itWhy a quantum computer does not help
Owning a coinYou know a secret whose hash is inside the coin. Spending proves you know it. There is no signature anywhere in a payment.Finding the secret means inverting a hash. Grover leaves about 124 bits of work.
Hiding a coinA coin on the ledger is a Poseidon2 hash of its amount, type, owner and a random 248-bit blind.No elliptic-curve commitment to break. Guessing the blind is a 124-bit search even with Grover.
Proving the rulesA STARK: a zero-knowledge proof built only from hash functions, with no trusted setup.There is no curve, no pairing and no setup secret in it for Shor's algorithm to attack.
Telling the recipientThe coin's details are encrypted to the recipient with ML-KEM-768, the NIST standard FIPS 203.ML-KEM is a lattice scheme, chosen by NIST to withstand quantum attack.
Issuing a coinA coin's issuers sign each mint with ML-DSA-65, the NIST standard FIPS 204, as an M-of-N group.ML-DSA is the lattice signature that goes with ML-KEM.

None of this was added later. The chain started this way on 28 October 2026. Every coin that has ever existed on ZCrypt is post-quantum, and there is no migration to wait for.

03

Record now, decrypt later.

A blockchain is public and permanent. Anyone can copy it today and keep the copy until better machines exist. On a chain that hides payments with elliptic curves, that copy becomes readable the day a large quantum computer runs: past amounts and past recipients, for every payment ever made.

This is why the encryption has to be post-quantum from the first block, not from some later upgrade. A payment recorded on ZCrypt today is encrypted with ML-KEM and committed with hashes, so the copy stays closed.

We claim about 124 bits against a quantum attacker trying to invert a hash, which is the attack that would take a coin. We do not claim 128-bit post-quantum collision resistance. These figures treat the hash function as ideal; they are not a proven lower bound on Poseidon2 itself.

Check the figures yourself

The node prints every one of these numbers from its compiled constants:

zcrypt --proof-params

Proofs use FRI with 112 queries and 20 bits of proof of work over the BabyBear field, with Poseidon2 as the only hash.

05

What is not post-quantum.

  • Block signing. Validators sign blocks with ed25519, as every CometBFT chain does. A quantum attacker who broke it could disrupt or stall block production. It could not forge a coin, spend someone else's, or read a payment, because none of those depend on a signature.
  • Bitcoin itself. Bitcoin held in the bridge reserve is protected by Bitcoin's own secp256k1 signatures, like all Bitcoin.
  • Two bridge signatures. When Bitcoin comes in, a secp256k1 signature from the depositor chooses who receives the zBTC, and another kind rotates the reserve key. A quantum forgery of those keys could redirect a deposit being minted or rotate the reserve. It could not create zBTC without a real Bitcoin deposit.
  • The building blocks are assumptions. Poseidon2, ML-KEM and ML-DSA are believed secure against quantum attack. Nobody can prove that about any cryptography, classical or post-quantum.

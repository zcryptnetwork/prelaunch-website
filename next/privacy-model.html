Privacy model

What the world can see.

Exactly what a payment puts on the public record, what is public on purpose, and where the limits are. You can check it: paste any payment into the explorer and it shows every byte.

01

What a payment publishes.

On the chainWhat it is
2 nullifiersMarks that two coins are now spent. They do not say which coins.
3 commitmentsThree new sealed coins. Always three, even when fewer are needed.
1 tree rootA recent snapshot of the pool the spent coins belong to.
3 encrypted notesThe details of the new coins, readable only by their owners. Every note is the same size.
A fee0.01 ZCRYPT per transaction, destroyed, and shown in the open. The wallet pays exactly 0.01 unless it is clearing out a leftover smaller than the fee, in which case the fee is slightly higher and that figure is visible. A payment file takes three transactions in all, 0.03.
A proofAbout 2 MB showing the rules were followed.

Nothing else is recorded: no sender, no recipient, no amount, no link to any earlier payment.

One thing does show: whether the payment was in ZCRYPT or in some other coin. Which other coin stays hidden.

Under the hood

A ZCRYPT transfer (0x6A) is one proof whose first output is the fee burn, opened publicly. A transfer of any other asset (0x55) is a bundle of two proofs: the payment, with a zero fee, and a ZCRYPT fee proof. Notes are ML-KEM-768 ciphertexts with a padded payload, and consensus enforces the size.

02

Hidden among every coin.

Many private coins pick a handful of decoys for each payment. Over time the decoy sets overlap, and analysis narrows them down.

ZCrypt picks no decoys. A spend proves that its coin is somewhere in the whole pool, so it could be any coin ever created. There is no public mode running alongside, so every user adds to every other user's cover.

The shape is the same too. A payment that spends one coin is padded to look exactly like one that spends two.

Under the hood

The circuit contains a Merkle membership proof from the spent coin's commitment to the published root, 62 levels deep. A one-input spend carries a value-zero phantom input whose nullifier is real and derived from the real input, so one-input and two-input transfers are byte-identical in shape.

03

Why a spend cannot be traced back.

When a coin is created, the chain stores a hash of it. When the coin is spent, the chain stores a different hash that only the owner's secret can produce. Nobody else can compute the second from the first, so nobody can watch a coin and see it spent.

That holds even for a coin whose creation was public, such as a staking reward: the first time it is spent, the trail ends.

Under the hood
pk = Poseidon2(spend_secret)
C  = Poseidon2(value, Poseidon2(pk, blind), asset_id)
nf = Poseidon2(Poseidon2(spend_secret, blind), value, asset_id)

The nullifier is deterministic per coin, so a double-spend collides and is rejected. The blind is eight field limbs, about 248 bits. The asset id is inside the commitment, and the circuit enforces conservation per asset, so hiding the type never lets one coin turn into another.

04

Three ways to be paid.

KindWhat it isRestored by your phrase
Normal addressYour reusable address. The wallet finds payments to it by scanning the chain.Yes
Shielded addressA fresh address that is not tied to your normal one. Use it for anything you do not want linked to the address you hand out.The address, yes. The wallet moves each coin received there into your phrase-backed balance on its next sync.
Payment fileA text file that is the money. No recipient is named anywhere.No. The file is the only copy.

A payment file can be claimed by anyone holding a copy until the first claim is in a block. Treat it like a banknote that can be photographed.

05

Sharing with an accountant or auditor.

You can give someone a view key. It lets them read the payments that arrived at your normal address: the amount and the coin of each one.

  • They cannot spend anything.
  • They cannot see whether you later spent a coin, or where it went.
  • They cannot see shielded addresses, change, or payment files.

Only you can hand out a view key. Nobody else holds one.

Limits: a view key is permanent for that address, so to stop sharing you move to a new address. There is no key yet that shows only one payment or only a period of time, and none that shows outgoing payments.

06

Public on purpose.

Some things are public so that other people can check them.

WhatWhat is visibleWhat stays hidden
StakingStakes, delegations, reward payouts, and the key that claims themWhich coins funded the stake. A reward is private again once it is spent.
Foundation vestingEvery claim against the scheduleWhere the coins go afterwards
A swap or bridge escrowThe amount, the coin, and both parties' keys for that one escrowWhich coins funded it, and the later spend of what it pays out
The Bitcoin reserveEvery deposit and withdrawal, on Bitcoin, to one known reserve keyWhat the zBTC does inside the pool

Two things to do. A coin keeps its exact amount until a payment splits it, so Bitcoin brought in and taken straight back out can be matched by its amount: make an ordinary payment in between. And every public action above names the key it acts for, so use a shielded address for anything you do not want tied to the address you give people.

07

The network around the chain.

  • Tor is on by default in the desktop wallet. If the Tor proxy is missing, the wallet refuses to connect; it does not fall back to a direct connection.
  • The wallet never asks a node about your coins. It downloads blocks and works out what is yours, and what you have spent, on your own device.
  • Use an HTTPS node. The wallet refuses plain HTTP to a remote node. The public node at https://rpc.zcrypt.network is HTTPS.

08

Where the limits are.

  • The crowd is as big as the pool. On a young chain the mathematics is the same but the crowd is small. It grows with use.
  • Timing can identify you. If you are the only one active in a quiet hour, your payments stand out.
  • A payment file is a copyable secret. Anyone with a copy can claim it until the first claim lands.
  • Notes use post-quantum encryption. ML-KEM-768 is used so that data recorded today cannot be opened by a future quantum computer.

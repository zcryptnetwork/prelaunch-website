Make your own coin

Your own coin. No code.

You do not need Solidity, Vyper or any programming language to issue a coin on ZCrypt. Choose a ticker, set a supply and click Mint. From that moment the coin belongs to you.

01

No smart contract to write.

On most networks a new coin is a program. Someone has to write it in Solidity or Vyper, compile it, deploy it and pay for the deployment. If the program has a bug, the coin can be drained or frozen, which is why serious projects pay for audits before they launch.

On ZCrypt a coin is not a program. Issuing coins is built into the network itself, and every coin follows the same rules as ZCRYPT. There is nothing to write, so there is nothing to get wrong.

03

The coin is yours.

The right to mint your coin lives in your wallet and nowhere else. It is derived from your recovery phrase, so there is no admin account, no company and no server that could mint it for you or take it from you.

Only you can mint

Every mint must be signed by your key. Anyone else who tries is rejected by every validator.

The supply is fixed

The ceiling you chose is written into the chain when the coin is registered. It cannot be raised later by anyone.

As private as ZCRYPT

Payments in your coin hide the sender, the receiver and the amount, in the same pool as every other coin.

Tradable from day one

Your coin can be swapped for any other coin on ZCrypt, directly between two people. See how trading works

04

What to know first.

  • Issuing is in the desktop wallet, under the Mint tab. Coins you issue can be held and sent from the phone wallet too.
  • Keep your recovery phrase. It is the only thing that can mint more of your coin. If you lose it, the coins already issued keep working, but no more can ever be made.
  • Issuing is public, paying is private. The ticker, the supply ceiling and the amount of each mint are visible on the chain. Payments made with the coin afterwards are not.
  • Fees are paid in ZCRYPT. Sending your coin costs 0.01 ZCRYPT, so holders need a little ZCRYPT to move it.
  • Some names are taken. ZCRYPT, zBTC and a short list of names kept for network assets cannot be registered.
  • Every coin has 8 decimal places, like Bitcoin, so amounts mean the same thing everywhere.
Under the hood
  • Registering a coin adds one entry to an on-chain registry: the ticker (case-insensitive), 8 decimals, the hard cap and a commitment to the issuer's key set.
  • The issuer's keys are ML-DSA-65, the NIST post-quantum signature standard. Each mint carries a signature over the exact amount and recipient, so a signature cannot be replayed.
  • The chain keeps a supply counter for every coin and rejects a mint that would pass the cap.
  • A coin's type is hidden inside each commitment, and the payment proof enforces that a transfer never changes one coin into another.
  • A group can share the mint right: the registry supports M-of-N key sets. The wallet creates a 1-of-1 set for you.
